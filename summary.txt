Wednesday, 11 November 2026. The day's biggest story was in Abu Dhabi, where two days of US-mediated talks between Russia and Ukraine ended with an agreement on a major prisoner exchange but no breakthrough on territory or a ceasefire, Reuters and AP reported. US envoy Jared Kushner called it the biggest humanitarian prisoner release yet and said the numbers would be finalised Thursday; Russia's Vladimir Medinsky and Ukraine's Rustem Umerov both confirmed the deal. Zelenskyy, who did not attend, had spoken of 1,000 to 1,500 prisoners each side. Kushner and Steve Witkoff are due to meet Putin and Zelenskyy separately on Thursday.

The Iran file moved in several directions at once. At the G7 foreign ministers' meeting in Paris, Secretary of State Marco Rubio said US sanctions and a naval blockade were working well enough that there was "no need" for major combat operations against Iran, the New York Times reported, while adding Washington could still use force if Iran crossed red lines. Iran's security chief Mohsen Rezaei told Lebanese TV that not a single particle of enriched nuclear material would leave the country and that any new US strike would be answered by targeting US bases far away, Reuters reported. Iran's Foreign Ministry also designated Argentina's navy and air force as terrorist organisations, a reciprocal move after Argentina listed the Revolutionary Guards in April. The US Justice Department charged three men it says were Iran-backed operatives in plots in the US and Europe, most recently at RAF Fairford in Britain; Iran denied involvement, the BBC reported.

In Washington, House Republicans blocked impeachment charges against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth by a 211-204 procedural vote, with 11 Democrats absent; Trump called the outcome an exoneration, Democracy Now reported. House Republicans elected Jim Jordan as their leader, replacing Speaker Mike Johnson in January when a new Democratic majority of at least 256 seats takes office, and Senate Republicans reelected John Thune. Democrats hold 54 Senate seats, with Alaska and Mississippi still leaning their way. Bernie Sanders said he will not back Chuck Schumer for Senate majority leader, joined by Elissa Slotkin and several senators-elect; Chris Van Hollen signalled he is open to running himself.

Elsewhere, Israeli forces killed at least seven people in Gaza, including a father and his 13-year-old son in a strike on a displacement shelter in Zeitoun, Gaza City, medics told Reuters, while the IDF said it struck militants. In the West Bank, soldiers killed 14-year-old Moataz Iyad Daghlas during a raid on Burqa. At COP31 in Turkey, which opened without the United States, UN Secretary-General Antonio Guterres told leaders the climate crisis is outrunning the world and called the current path one of mutually assured destruction, Reuters reported. Greta Thunberg called the summit hypocritical, and Australia, Bangladesh, Kenya, Nigeria and Vanuatu backed a declaration to double rooftop solar homes by 2030. Nvidia said it hired former acting US attorney Joon H. Kim to investigate CEO Jensen Huang after reports of a relationship with a woman US intelligence believes spied for China, and the company lost about $430 billion in value, Principia reported. A federal judge let Trump's defamation suit against the BBC proceed over a spliced edit of his 6 January 2021 speech, POLITICO reported. In markets, Anthropic set an IPO price range implying a valuation near $1.4 trillion, and a boat capsized off Senegal killing at least 39.
